# 2026-08-25 — T2 MoE screening arms on an L4 (the seven flagship-preset 30-minute arms)

Launched 2026-08-24 22:25 as four on-demand Lightning jobs (`mote-t2-{0..3}-08242225`, GCP L4 23 GB, two arms per
job, account A; scratchpad `t2_l4_jobs.py`). Total cost **$1.885** (0.54 + 0.53 + 0.53 + 0.29 credits) against the
~$1.9 estimate. Every arm finished its 30-minute budget with a final eval; every job then **failed at its last
command** — `mkdir -p /teamspace/jobs/<name>/work` is not writable from inside a job (the SDK's `artifact_path` is
`/teamspace/jobs/<name>/artifacts`) — so the run directories never left the machines. The trainer prints every
log record to stdout, so the job logs carried each arm's complete `log.jsonl`; `t2_l4_recover.py` split them per
arm into `runs/t2_*/log.jsonl` (no checkpoints). Recipe: `--preset flagship --data data/local_mix --optimizer muon
--batch-size 1 --grad-accum 8 --seq-len 4096 --ckpt-main --max-minutes 30 --eval-every 300`, lr 8e-4 unless noted,
MoE = top-2 of half-size experts in every Relation layer (docs/shape.md § "Mixture of experts").

## What T2 was for, and what it says

T2 screens **throughput, memory and balance** (the funnel: quality is T3's question — "short arms are blind to MoE",
crossover ≈ 3.7 tokens/param).

* **Throughput**: at batch 1 × 4096 on the L4, E4 runs at **0.68–0.69×** the dense KB/s and E8 at **0.56–0.62×**.
  The expert `grouped_mm` over ~2 k tokens per micro-batch is launch-bound; batch 1 is the flagship's own regime on
  the 8 GB card, so this is the cost that matters.
* **Memory**: **+1.3 GB for E4, +3.2–3.5 GB for E8** over the dense 2.04 GB peak at this shape. The parameter-only
  estimate (+0.68 / +2.0 GB from 12 B/param) missed the expert activations and grouped-GEMM workspace by 0.7 / 1.4
  GB. Scaled to the frozen flagship shape (dense 4.31 GB at 16384): E4 ≈ 6.0–6.5 GB, E8 ≈ 8 GB. **E8 is out on the
  4060 Ti** (ceiling ≈ 7.3 GB); E4 fits only because serving now leaves the GPU while a job runs
  (docs/results/2026-08-24-web-qa.md).
* **Balance**: the aux router is balanced from the start (MaxVio 0.13–0.23 at step 300, 0.04–0.08 at the end); the
  lossfree router starts badly (0.6–2.1 at step 300 — the bias needs a few hundred steps) and converges to
  0.09–0.35. Layer-0-dense made E8's balance worse (0.35), not better.
* **Quality, for what a 30-minute arm is worth**: per step, every MoE arm beats dense (step 600: e4_lf 2.201, e4_aux
  2.230, d0 2.095 vs dense 2.301); per wall-clock, dense still wins at 30 minutes (1.929 vs the best MoE 1.952,
  with the tax against dense) — exactly the short-horizon blindness the funnel expected. **Lossfree beats aux at
  equal time at both sizes** (E4 −0.038, E8 −0.021 bpb) despite the worse balance, as DeepSeek-V3 claims (no
  aux-loss gradient interference); lr 5.7e-4 helped E8 by 0.022 at matched steps (543 vs 545).

**Carried into T3** (the 35M 12-hour arms, queued locally): the **lossfree router**; **E4 as the local MoE candidate**,
E8 excluded from the flagship by memory (its 35M T3 arm still runs — it fits at that size — for the scaling
read). The lr-vs-horizon fit stays the gate on the frozen lr; Noah sees it first.

## Lessons for the next cloud job

* Write artifacts to `/teamspace/jobs/<name>/artifacts` (the SDK's `artifact_path`), never `…/work`; the job's cwd
  is a snapshot of the studio, so nothing written under it survives.
* Warm the kernels before `--max-minutes` starts counting (one throwaway step, or a `--warm-first` flag).
* Keep the trainer's stdout records: they were the whole result this time.
