## What the logs say

**The freeze's lr gate is a throughput confound.** `runs/lr_sweep_*` (flagship preset, 30 min, 1×8 @4096):

| step | 3e-4 | 5e-4 | 8e-4 | 12e-4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 3.992 | 3.138 | 3.878 | 3.865 |
| 600 | 2.428 | 2.258 | 2.282 | 2.347 |
| 900 | 2.111 | 2.118 | 2.078 | **1.947** |
| final (steps) | 1.716 (2567) | 1.707 (2161) | 1.693 (2555) | 1.935 (**923**) |

The 12e-4 arm led at every matched step and ran at 19.8 KB/s against 49 for the others, for the whole run,
so it got 36 % of the steps. Its router had recovered by step 400 (bpic 3.4, grad-norm 30). "12e-4
destabilized the router" is not what happened. The norm guard's "`lr_sweep_12e-4` signature" (shape.md)
rests on that reading; the guard has never tripped, so its calibration is untested either way.

**The step-50 router collapse is universal.** All four arms — including 3e-4 at lr 6e-5 — emit ~1 boundary
per sequence around step 50 (bpic 1,900–4,100, ce 9–19, grad-norm 900–2,100) and recover by step 150–400.
The 35M T3 arms show the same (bpic 12–72 at step 100). It is not an lr threshold and not a Newton–Schulz
depth effect; every run pays ~100–150 steps for it.

**At 12 h, lower is better.** `t3l_dense_{4,8,16}e-4` (local 35M, 4×4 @2048, `--schedule trunk`,
`--eval-ema 0.9999`, 720 min): 1.1029 / 1.1265 / 1.1817.

**The signed horizon fit** (`mote.train.lr_horizon`, Kakao 2608.20061 §2.2.1; `2026-08-28-lr-horizon-t3.json`):
ln lr* = 2.124 − 0.437 ln D, R² 0.984 over 66 budgets; lr* = 4.7e-4 at the 12-h budget (8e-4 sits
+0.0095 bpb above it) and **2.25e-4 at the trunk's 2.84e10 bytes** (7 days at 47 KB/s, a 6× extrapolation
in D). The parabola prices 8e-4 at ~0.05 bpb and 4e-4 at ~0.01 above the 7-day optimum.

**Every lr number is at 32 KB per optimizer step**; the frozen trunk (1×4 @16384) is 64 KB/step.

## Addendum (2026-08-28, 19:45): a throughput confound on `elr_gate/muon_ref`

A CPU-only evaluation sweep (recirculation, `scripts/recirc_sweep.py`, 16 then 2×14 threads) ran beside
`muon_ref` from elapsed 83 to 94.5 min. The arm's `bytes_per_sec` fell from a steady 110.6 KB/s (elapsed
60–83, n=466) to a mean of 93 KB/s (−16 %, troughs to 62) over that window, and was back at 110.5 KB/s
within a minute of the stop (elapsed 94.5–96.5, n=23). `muon_ref` is
`--max-minutes 120` with no step cap, so it will end ~2 % short of the steps it would have reached.
**Read the ELR pair at matched steps** (`scripts/elr_report.py` does), and treat any wall-clock reading of
this pair as biased against Muon by that amount. Standing rule from this: no CPU-heavy work beside a
wall-clock-budgeted arm; the recirculation grid finishes on an idle queue.
