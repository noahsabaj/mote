Decisions: **Muon adopted** (SW decided on a longer run); **ratio weight stays 0.1**; **β₂ stays 0.95**;

(acceptance 12 % default / 4 % mbp2, both auto-paused) — provided the window cap (below) makes it cheap.
AdamW arms ran with tail grad norms of 34–47 against clip 1.0, so AdamW-based comparisons are noisy; the
Muon lab queue re-runs the head arms, a `muon_2048` repeat (noise floor) and a 4096 arm at equal steps.

## Profiles

The chain's own profiles ran a **random router** (~9 chunks / 2048 bytes) and are not the training regime;
kept only as the overhead floor: old path 8.3 % MFU → bucketing 8.7 % → FlashRelation 9.0 % (peak memory
3.38 → 1.65 GB). Block-local MBP at batch 4 hit 0.9 % — a random 2 KB chunk inflates its key window for
the whole batch; the same pathology appears in from-scratch training whenever the router collapses.

Trained router (`--init-from runs/overnight/last.pt`, 3.24 bytes/chunk):

| config | B/s | TFLOPS | MFU | peak GB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old path (no flash, exact, dense MBP), 2048 b2 | 42.7 K | 5.5 | 12.4 % | 2.65 |
| flash + bucket, dense MBP, 2048 b2 | 69.8 K | 8.9 | 20.3 % | 1.69 |
| flash + bucket, block-local MBP, 2048 b2 | 71.2 K | 9.1 | 20.7 % | 1.70 |
| same, batch 4 | **80.8 K** | 10.4 | 23.5 % | 2.70 |
| same, batch 4, ckpt-main | 74.7 K | 9.6 | 21.7 % | 2.03 |
| no head, 2048 b2 | 64.7 K | 5.6 | 12.7 % | 1.40 |
| 4096 b2 ckpt | 72.9 K | 11.4 | 25.8 % | 2.03 |
| 8192 b1 ckpt | 65.7 K | 14.0 | 31.7 % | 2.07 |
| 16384 b1 ckpt | 63.7 K | 20.8 | 47.2 % | 3.32 |

Flagship preset (105M), random weights, forced 6 bytes/chunk, batch 1, ckpt-main:

| window | B/s | TFLOPS | MFU | peak GB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4096 | 28.9 K | 7.7 | 17.5 % | 3.06 |
| 8192 | 42.3 K | 13.9 | 31.6 % | 4.17 |
| **16384** | **42.4 K** | 19.3 | 43.7 % | **6.34** |

16384 fits the 8 GB card → the flagship preset is 16384. At 42 KB/s the 9.88 GB mix is ~2.7 days per
epoch. The speed round delivered 1.7–1.9× per byte at 2048 on the trained router; the ≥ 25 % MFU bar is
met from 4096 up. Caveat: from-scratch training passes through router collapse and spiky chunk lengths
that the trained-router profile never sees — the block-local window cap is what makes training match it.
