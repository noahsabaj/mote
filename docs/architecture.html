The case for Relation over attention

Attention computes one scalar per pair, U_ij = q_i·k_j/√d, and turns that same scalar straight into flow with a softmax. One number has to encode both how two tokens relate and how much should move between them. Relation (Ge, Yang & Nie, 2026) separates the two: pairwise evidence is first organized into a token's relation with itself and its relations with others, and the flow is derived afterwards.

S_i = σ(U_ii / τ_S) E_ij = SiLU(U_ij) R_ij = S_i (j=i) | E_ij − λ_ℓ·log i (j<i) | −∞ (j>i) F_i = softmax(R_i) Y_i = Σ_j F_ij Ĩ_j

Three things follow that attention does not give you:

Self vs. othersImplicit; the diagonal competes on the same scale as everything else.Explicit and exact: F_ii = σ(S_i − A_i), g_i = 1 − F_ii. The model first decides whether to rely on itself, then where to look.
Length dilutionMore history means more candidates sharing the mass; needs tricks like attention sinks or logit scaling.Count calibration −λ_ℓ log i corrects the aggregate exchange evidence for how many predecessors exist, per layer, learned.
What you can read offAn attention map.A per-token, per-layer gate g_i — "ask self" vs. "ask others" — which the studio shows live.
CostO(T²d); FlashAttention kernels everywhere.Same complexity and an exact tiled form (FlashRelation at 76–85% of FlashAttention throughput); a linear recurrent form exists too. Cache is {P₂, Ĩ}, same size as KV.
EvidenceEverything.Lower validation NLL than MHA at 10M, 30M and 100M params in the paper (−0.041, −0.015, −0.031 nats); the 10M setting is exactly our main network's shape. Our pilot trained it without a single instability.

Why it fits this model in particular: the main network sees chunks, not bytes. A chunk is a small unit of meaning, so "should this chunk draw on earlier chunks or stand on its own" is a real question, and the gate answers it. Measured on the pilot checkpoint for one reply, the exchange mass per layer was:

L10.84 L20.77 L30.76 L40.89 L50.94 L60.99

Deeper layers draw almost entirely on history; the first layers keep a quarter of their mass on the chunk itself.

The honest counter-case: one paper, small scales, no ecosystem, and at our sequence lengths (a few hundred chunks) the efficiency argument is moot either way. The bet is on the inductive bias and the interpretability, and the thing that would falsify it — Relation failing to scale — is exactly what the 35M and 100M runs will show.

Training objective

L = CE(next byte) + λ₁·CE(multi-byte head) + α·L_ratio, L_ratio = N/(N−1)·[(N−1)·F·G + (1−F)(1−G)]

F is the fraction of bytes the router kept, G its mean boundary probability; the loss is minimal when both equal 1/N. The straight-through gate on the dechunked output lets the router learn from the language loss, and the EMA keeps every step differentiable.
