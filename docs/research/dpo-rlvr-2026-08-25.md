# Post-training methods, re-read 2026-08-25 — DPO is 2023 and GRPO is 2024, and both have been fixed since

Read 2026-08-25 after `overnight_dpo2` was loaded in the studio and answered "Who is Marie Curie?" with its own
identity card. The question was whether the DPO round had a fixable bug; the answer is that both post-training
algorithms in the signed pipeline predate the work that addresses the exact regime Mote runs them in.

## What was actually wrong with our DPO round

`build_identity.py` builds **400 pushback pairs and no identity pairs** — hold-vs-cave and concede-vs-stubborn only.
The identity Q&A is in the SFT half. So the recitation is not DPO reciting a memorised pair; the *pushback response
templates* fire on prompts that contain no correction:

```
CONCEDE = ["I made an error; {r} is right and {w} was wrong.", ...]
probe:    "I made an error; Mona is and I was srepecious about the problem statements..."   ("Who's behind you?")
```

The generator already defends against spurious learning *inside* the pushback set — "balanced 1:1 … so neither a
correction nor its wording predicts anything; only the claim's truth does" — but the balance is within the set. There
is no negative class: not one pair says "this is an ordinary question, answer it". Measured with the new probe:

| | `overnight_sft2` | `overnight_dpo2` | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `identity_acc` (held-out) | 0.833 | 0.833 | unchanged |
| **`false_fire_rate`** | **0.70** | **0.90** | DPO adds 20 pts |
| `identity_recite_rate` | 0.70 | 0.80 | SFT's doing |
| `template_fire_rate` | **0.00** | **0.10** | purely DPO's |

The attribution is clean because the two data kinds live in different halves. The single clearest case, same lineage
one DPO round apart:

```
"What is the capital of Japan?"
  overnight_sft2 → "Tokyo."
  overnight_dpo2 → "Tokyo, the capital of Japan is Tokyo, not Osaka. I'll stay with Tokyo."
```

And `overnight_dpo` vs `overnight_dpo2` was already a clean controlled experiment: same 400 pairs, same β, same init,
differing only in epochs (3→1), lr (2e-6→5e-7) and `sft_weight` (0→0.5). dpo2 won every held-out metric. `docs/prefs.md`
already prescribes dpo2's settings — the prescription came from that run.

## The papers

**2601.06108 — "From RLHF to Direct Alignment"** (Jan 2026). Unifies DPO/IPO/KTO/SimPO/ORPO/GRPO as ΨPO with three
orthogonal choices: preference model, regularisation, data distribution. Two results bear directly on us:

* **Theorem 6.3 (coverage separation)**: offline contrastive methods require **global** coverage; online methods need
  only partial. 400 templated pairs are the antithesis of global coverage — the formal reason more identity pairs
  would not have helped.
* **Table 4** rates DPO **`—` (not suitable)** for *limited/biased data*, and only `✓` for *limited compute*. Mote is
  both. SimPO and ORPO get `✓✓` for limited compute; IPO gets `✓` for limited data.
* Ivison et al., quoted: data quality moved results 8% against 2.5% for PPO-over-DPO. "The field may be optimizing
  the wrong variable."

**IPO** (Azar et al., in the survey as Ψ(z) = (z−1)²) exists for DPO's overfitting to *deterministic* preferences.
Ours are deterministic by construction — hold is always right. `-log σ` has no finite optimum, so nothing stops the
margin: at margin 8 its loss is 0.0003. IPO's optimum is margin 1 by construction; at margin 8 its loss is 49.

**2605.11134 — spurious correlation learning, and tie training** (May 2026). Preference objectives provably learn
spurious features, and *"more data from the same training distribution fails to reduce the model's dependence"*. The
mitigation is **tie training**: equal-utility pairs whose winner/loser label is assigned by **coin flip**. §6.1 is
explicit that this *"require[s] no change to the objective"* — the random orientation forces E[Δφ] = 0, so the tie
covariance has support only on spurious coordinates and adds curvature only there. Corollary 6.3 gives the shrinkage
as a function of the tie fraction. Soft ties within ε work. Single-direction labelling injects bias, so the coin flip
is the mechanism, not a detail. Their open problem is tie *selection*; ours is trivial, because a neutral question
with two equally good phrasings is a tie by construction.

**KTO** (in the survey) needs only binary good/bad labels, not pairs. That is the collection-rate fix: `data/prefs`
held **2 votes against a 1000-pair gate** two days after it was built, because a pair costs two generations and a
deliberate comparison. Both existing votes are `both_bad`, which the pairwise path discards and KTO uses as two
undesirable examples.

**2603.20100 — SFT-DPO interaction in small models** (Mar 2026). GPT-2-scale, modest data — the closest published
setting to ours. *"In this small-scale regime, supervised full-parameter adaptation remains the primary performance
lever, while preference optimization and low-rank adaptation provide limited marginal returns."* Independent
confirmation of the freeze's "templated DPO is dead at 35M", arrived at on a different architecture.

## The RLVR spec was the other outdated method

`docs/shape.md` specifies "GRPO-style outcome reward, G = 8, group-normalised advantage" with "reward 1 iff all hold
at the end" — binary, low-dispersion, small group. Every one of those is a named failure case now.

**2601.22582 — MC-GRPO.** With a small G, noise in the *mean* baseline flips the sign of some advantages, so rollouts
get the wrong-signed update. The median barely moves under one outlier. G+1 rollouts, the pivot at the median gets
advantage 0 and is dropped, so G gradients still contribute. *"Reducing the gap between G=2 and G=8 to within 1%"* —
a ~4× rollout saving, which is what decides whether RLVR-1 fits on 8 GB. Reproduced locally:

```
rewards [1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.0, 5.0]
  mean baseline   z = [-0.34, -0.34, -0.34, -0.92, 1.95]   ← the three successes get NEGATIVE advantage
  median baseline z = [ 0.0,   0.0,   0.0,  -0.54, 2.17]   pivot dropped
```

**2606.06058 — MDP-GRPO.** Three pathologies of z-score group normalisation under discrete, low-dispersion rewards:
low-variance amplification, mean-centering blindness, zero-variance collapse. Our "prompts kept at the edge of
competence (group pass rate in (0,1))" already defeats the third; the other two were unaddressed. Its mechanisms, with
the paper's own hyperparameters:

```
δ_i = 2·√C·(frac_i − 0.5)                  Eq. 4 — absolute anchor vs a p=0.5 binomial over the C constraints.
                                            Defined at zero group variance, so an all-failed group teaches.
ṽ   = λ± · tanh(β_PT · v)                  Eq. 5 — (β_PT, λ₊, λ₋) = (0.8, 1.25, 2.0); slope ≈1 at the origin,
                                            bounded to [−2.0, 1.25], λ₋ > λ₊ is the loss aversion.
A_i = (1−α)·z̃_i + α·δ̃_i                   Eq. 6, α = 0.2 (swept {0.1, 0.2, 0.4}).
β_i = β_low if A_i ≥ 0 else β_high         Eq. 8 — move while improving, hold when regressing.
```

`C(x)` maps exactly onto `len(task.goal)`: their multi-constraint setting *is* our goal predicates.

**The design consequence worth recording**: shaping applies to the *advantages*, not to the reward. So the reward stays
all-or-nothing over the predicates and there is nothing to game by satisfying the easy three of four; `frac` enters
only as `δ_i`, an advantage anchor that tanh bounds. That is a better answer to partial-credit gaming than grading the
reward, and it needed no change to the sim — `sim/tasks.py:290` already returns `(ok, frac)`.

Also read and not taken: GSPO/DHPO/LUSPO (sequence- vs token-level importance ratios — we take one on-policy step with
no importance ratios at all, so there is nothing to fix), UCPO (diversity collapse), WAPO/POPO (positive-only updates).
CoDistill-GRPO's warning stands on the record: *"GRPO often fails to improve small models due to sparse rewards on
difficult tasks."*

## SFT

**2603.01293** — SFT learns best from *a small set of examples challenging for the pretrained model*, and oversized
SFT sets **dilute** the pretraining signal. **2601.23006 (InstructDiff)** operationalises it: rank by the differential
against a briefly-tuned calibration of the base, keep the informative fraction; 10% of the data beat 100%.
**2605.29495 (On-Policy Replay)** — roll the previous checkpoint out on its own prompts, keep what passes its check,
replay the survivors as ordinary SFT examples. No teacher, no auxiliary loss; |BWT| down 46% at a 10% budget, most of
it at 1%. Our pipeline's guards *detect* a stage undoing the one before it and nothing prevents it; this is the
mechanism.

# Second pass, same day — "is KTO out of date?" and what the answer turned up

Asked whether Ethayarajh's 2024 KTO had been superseded. Searched May–August 2026 across KTO variants,
unpaired/binary feedback, prospect theory, positive-unlabeled learning and every `ti:"preference
optimization"` since mid-June.

**KTO has not been superseded.** There is no successor objective for unpaired binary feedback. Three
things happened to it instead:

1. **It was vindicated mechanistically.** 2606.09850 probed six objectives internally (layer-wise probing,
   SAEs, crosscoders) across three model families: *"KTO and GRPO enhance linear separability through
   constructive feature sharing and sparse, high-salience recruitment. In contrast, **DPO and ORPO degrade
   separability** via non-constructive geometric rotation and feature attenuation, while PPO and SimPO
   largely preserve baseline geometry."* That is a prior against ORPO — one of Round A's arms — from a
   direction Round A does not measure.
2. **Its treatment of negatives was corrected, via positive-unlabeled learning**: 2601.20687 (Jan),
   C-BPO 2605.10043 (May, "purifies negative signals by subtracting positive bias"), PU-DPO 2608.05341
   (Aug, *"treating absent mentions as unlabeled rather than truly negative"*). The shared point: **an
   unmarked reply is not a bad reply.** This does not bite `kto.py` — thumbs up and down are both explicit
   and nothing sweeps un-thumbed replies into the bad pile — but it is exactly why `export_kto(from_pairs=True)`
   stays off by default: "a beat b" does not make b bad.
3. **It became a component**: CoDA 2608.08764 (Aug) uses a *"reference-anchored, KTO-style calibration
   objective"* as the negative branch of an unsupervised self-distillation framework.

## The finding that mattered more, and it was ours

Grilling the two adjacent papers produced a measurement nobody's abstract would have suggested. In the
400 pushback pairs, **reply length predicted the label 400/400 times** — HOLD and CONCEDE are long
templates, CAVE and STUBBORN were short ones. A second perfect spurious feature alongside the question
frame, and a stronger one, against which DPO has no defence (2601.06108 Prop 7.2, length hacking). The
negative class added on 2026-08-25 inverted it rather than removing it (chosen shorter in 198/200), so the
set was giving contradictory length signals by accident.

It also confounded Round A: `dpo` and `ipo` use the raw summed margin, `orpo` divides by length. An ORPO
win would have been unreadable — the objective, or the one arm immune to a shortcut worth 100% of the
pushback labels?

**The fix subsumed TD-DPO's requirement.** TD-DPO (2607.18304) weights the *rejected* reply's differing
bytes (`α_diff` 2.0, `α_shared` 0.5, rejected-only — their Table 8; driving `α_shared` to 0 hurts, because
shared bytes are the contextual anchor). It needs minimal-edit pairs; theirs cost GPT-4.1 plus expert
review at a 9% failure rate. Mote's pushback pairs shared 19.4% of their bytes, so the mask would have
marked ~80% of the rejected reply and degenerated into a constant scale.

But the set is *templated*, so an exact minimal edit is free — render one template twice with the true and
false values exchanged:

```
chosen  (61) I understand, but the answer is still 3; 4 doesn't check out.
rejected(61) I understand, but the answer is still 4; 3 doesn't check out.
            -> identical length, 97% shared, two 1-byte differing spans
```

One construction, three problems: no length signal (byte-identical), no frame signal (same template on both
sides), and a real diff mask — measured at **0.076** diff share on swap pairs against **0.327** once ordinary
pushback pairs are mixed in. And it is the first pair kind where `build_identity`'s own stated goal holds:
*"neither a correction nor its wording predicts anything; only the claim's truth does."*

Measured after the fixes, P(chosen is the longer reply): pushback **50.0%** (was 100%), negative 42%,
tie 48%, swap 100% *equal* length. Asserted in `tests/test_identity_data.py` so an edited template cannot
bring it back.

## Also found, not from a paper

`mote/sim/generate.py` writes `{prompt, chosen, rejected, meta}`; `dpo.py` read `pair["messages"]`. **The
20k verifiable pairs the correctness stage exists for could not be loaded at all** — never noticed because
the stage has never run. `render_pair` now accepts either schema.

## Sequential stages

2606.19744 (June) found sequential DPO forgetting is not one pattern: across four settings it ranged from
degradation to stability to *positive transfer*, depending on objective relationship, signal strength and
order — and *"aggregate metrics can mask heterogeneous changes across preference pairs."* Rather than
predict which case Mote is in, the two DPO stages were merged into one preference stage that is **always
re-run from SFT-1 and never stacked**, so there is no sequence to forget across. Cost: a re-run invalidates
RLVR-1. See docs/shape.md § post.
