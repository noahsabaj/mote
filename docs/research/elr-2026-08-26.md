# 2026-08-26 — ELR: one coordinate under the learning rate, weight decay, and norm control

[2608.24814] "Effective Learning Rate Governs Loss Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining" (Liu, Zheng,
Zhang, Tian, Chen, Zhang, Wu — Peking University / Ant Group, 25 Aug 2026). Read in full: §1–8,
Appendices A–G, both tables.

## What the paper measures

Define ELR per matrix parameter as `η^eff = η / ‖W‖_F`. The claim is that **learning rate and parameter
norm are not two controls but one, and its scale**: construct runs whose LR and norm trajectories differ
substantially but whose ELR schedules match step by step, and the loss curves collapse.

The central experiment realizes one WSD ELR schedule four ways — WSD LR, linear-up, linear-down,
sinusoidal, each with a prescribed norm schedule that cancels the difference. Mean absolute loss
discrepancy from the reference: (1.8, 2.5, 2.6)×10⁻³ on Llama-124M, (3.0, 4.1, 3.3)×10⁻³ on
Qwen3-MoE-586M. Across 26 matched comparisons — Llama 124M/135M/1B, Qwen3 dense, Qwen3-MoE, Kimi Delta
Attention; FineWeb/C4/OpenWebText; AdamW/Muon/Signum — median 2.5×10⁻³, all under 5×10⁻³. A 9.8M ViT on
ImageNet-64 gives 3.16–3.19×10⁻³.

The calibration is what makes those numbers mean something. Re-running the identical configuration and
changing only the init seed costs 1.11×10⁻² nats; only the data-order seed, 1.62×10⁻². **Matching ELR
across wildly different LR schedules aligns loss 4.3–9× more tightly than re-running the same config under
a different seed.**

**It is not a symmetry theorem.** App. A.1 is careful: transformers are not scale-invariant — pre-norm
blocks treat identity and residual branches differently under rescaling, the V·O path scales as c², SwiGLU's
gate couples three matrices and SiLU is not homogeneous, and tied embeddings change both the residual
scale and the softmax temperature. Two conditions control the precision, and one is genuinely strange:

| ablation | collapse error |
|---|---|
| default (QK-Norm + learnable RMSNorm gains) | 2.3×10⁻³ |
| − QK-Norm | 5.2×10⁻³ |
| − QK-Norm, RMSNorm gains **fixed** | 1.84×10⁻² |

Fixing the gains makes the parameterization *more* scale-invariant and makes the collapse 3.5× worse. The
authors say plainly they do not have the mechanism. Same ordering reproduces in the ViT. The second
condition is timescale: modulating LR and target norm by the same sinusoid (ELR unchanged) takes the error
from 2.8×10⁻³ at 2 cycles to 7.5×10⁻³ at 32, with residuals ringing at the imposed frequency — so the
compensation is dynamical with a finite response time.

**Weight decay and Hyperball are ELR mechanisms.** Leave the norm control in place and adapt *only* the
LR to match a target ELR: AdamW λ=0.1 vs λ=0 → 4.8×10⁻³; MuonH (Hyperball) vs MuonW → 1.2×10⁻³; both
directions reverse cleanly. Fitting a functional scaling law on four non-Hyperball runs and evaluating on
two unseen Hyperball runs: elr-FSL RMSE 0.0212 vs lr-FSL 0.2508. (The abstract and Fig. 6's caption say
12.9×; the table and body say 11.83×, and 0.2508/0.0212 = 11.83. Internal inconsistency, no bearing.)

**Delayed acceleration.** A weight-decayed run sits above the unregularized baseline for most of training
and overtakes at the end. Without WD the norm grows steadily, so ELR decays much faster than the nominal
LR; WD restrains the growth, sustains a higher ELR, accumulates effective training time faster, and lowers
the signal term — while the η²-weighted noise term rises and masks it. During the decay phase new noise
stops arriving and old noise is forgotten, and the advantage surfaces. *Acquired early, revealed late.*
The decisive test (App. F.1): fix everything, vary only the WSD decay ratio. At 0.1 the λ=0.1 run never
overtakes. At 0.3 it does and finishes lower. Then they turn it into a control — keep the λ=0.3 ELR early
and drive ELR *down harder* late by **growing the parameter norm faster** — and beat the weight-decay
baseline. The benefit was never small norms; it was the ELR shape.

Scope limit they state themselves: loss dynamics only. Matched loss curves do not imply matched
parameters, representations, or downstream behaviour. And §8 poses cross-scale ELR transfer as **open**,
not answered.

## What it does to Mote — measured, not inferred

Every number below came from checkpoints already on disk. No GPU was used; the E4 MoE arm held the card.

### The trunk sits at the weight-decay norm equilibrium

`runs/t3l_dense_{4,8,16}e-4` are a natural experiment for this paper: identical config, identical seed 42,
720 min each, `--schedule trunk` (constant LR after warmup), Muon, wd 0.1 — only the LR differs.

| arm | lr | ‖W‖_F | ELR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4e-4 | 4.0e-4 | 265.76 | 1.505e-6 |
| 8e-4 | 8.0e-4 | 369.65 | 2.164e-6 |
| 16e-4 | 1.6e-3 | 516.46 | 3.098e-6 |

**‖W‖ ∝ lr^0.478** from both ratios (√lr predicts 0.5); all 61 matrices cluster 1.23–1.46 around the
predicted 1.414. Decoupled decay has a fixed point where the shrinkage `1−ηλ` balances Muon's fixed-norm
update, at `‖W‖ ∝ √(η/λ)`, and the arms are on it. Two consequences:

* **lr and weight decay are one axis.** At equilibrium ELR ∝ √(lr·λ), so (8e-4, 0.1) and (3.2e-3, 0.025)
  are the same run. Mote has never swept wd; under this it never needed to.
* **The LR sweep did not sweep what it looked like.** 4e-4 → 16e-4 is 4× in lr and **2.06× in ELR**.

### The nominal lr does transfer across width — but not because ELR does

Entrywise RMS of the Muon matrices, grouped by shape at lr 4e-4: 512×512 → 0.0394, 512×1536 → 0.0450,
3072×512 → 0.0472, 384×768 → 0.0557. A 1.4× spread across a 6× range in matrix size, i.e. ‖W‖_F ∝ √(mn).
Across arms the means are 0.0419 → 0.0580 → 0.0811, fitting **RMS/entry ≈ 0.66·√(lr/λ)** to 3%.

Flat RMS/entry makes the *relative* step `lr·‖U‖_F/‖W‖_F ≈ 0.30·√(lr·λ)` shape-independent — each Muon
matrix rotates ~0.27% of its own norm per step at lr 8e-4, wd 0.1, whether it is 512×512 or 768×2048. So
lr 8e-4 carries from the 35M lab arms to the flagship's d_model 768. ELR itself does **not** transfer:
`η/‖W‖_F` falls as √(mn). The transferable quantity is the relative step, and the horizon gap (0.2 → ~75
tokens/param) remains the real transfer risk, which is what `mote.train.lr_horizon` already targets.

### The optimizer gate is precise and unattributable

`runs/ab2_muon_2h` vs `runs/ab2_muonsw_2h` are the freeze's optimizer gate.

| | ‖W‖_F | ELR | val_bpb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muon, seed 42 | 302.00 | 2.649e-6 | 1.17734 |
| Muon-SW, seed 42 | 330.32 | 2.422e-6 | 1.18006 |
| Muon, seed 7 | — | — | 1.17758 |

**Muon-SW is not a different optimizer.** `mote/train/muon.py` differs in one line — the decay. At peak LR
the two decays are equal; below peak, Muon-SW's falls as η², so through warmup and the whole WSD tail it
decays less, which is exactly why its norm ended higher. It is a norm-control variant, and §5 is the
demonstration that comparing two norm-control methods at a matched nominal lr compares the ELRs they
induce. It ran at **0.914× Muon's ELR**.

Seed noise on final val_bpb is **0.00025 bpb**, so the 0.00272 bpb gap is 11× noise — the measurement is
precise. But the local slope `d(val_bpb)/d(ln ELR)` flips sign with horizon (−0.073 bpb/nat at 30 min from
the 5e-4→8e-4 secant, +0.026 at 12 h from 4e-4→8e-4), so an 0.0899-nat ELR gap explains **0.000 to 0.0066
bpb** of a 0.00272 bpb effect. The confound spans zero to 2.4× the effect. (The `lr_sweep_12e-4` arm is
excluded from the slope: its ‖W‖ fell to 221.5, *below* the 8e-4 arm's 294.6 at 1.5× the lr — a norm that
left its equilibrium, matching "12e-4 destabilized the router".)

### Seed noise, both metrics

`ab2_muon_2h` vs `ab2_muon_seed7` differ only in seed:

* the paper's trajectory metric (EMA 0.99, mean |ΔL| over post-warmup steps): **0.0126 bpb = 0.0088 nats**,
  inside their 1.11e-2 / 1.62e-2 band;
* Mote's own endpoint metric (final val_bpb): **0.00025 bpb**.

The two differ 50×, and the endpoint metric is the tighter one — which is the defence of how Mote's gates
(0.003, 0.005 bpb) are written, now measured rather than assumed. Two details change the trajectory number
by an order of magnitude and are recorded in `scripts/elr_report.py`: seed the EMA *at* the post-warmup cut
(the first logged step differs by 26 bpb between runs, and a 100-point EMA carries that ~500 points in —
it reported 2.7 bpb before the fix), and keep only the last fresh-start segment of `log.jsonl`.

### QK-Norm in Relation is a smaller change than it looked

`mote/model/relation.py` did the evidence product on raw projections. Adding QK-Norm is mechanically free —
normalizing after the projections and before RoPE means the Triton kernel and the arena both receive
already-normalized `p2` — but it is not loss-neutral, because `u = p1·p2ᵀ/√dh` feeds `silu(u) − λ·log i`
and `sigmoid(u/τ_s)`, neither of which is scale-covariant the way softmax is.

Measured on `runs/t3l_dense_4e-4` after 12 h, feeding the trained `w1`/`w2` an RMS-normalized stream (what
pre-norm hands the mixer):

```
u std per layer, unnormalised   1.37 1.17 0.91 0.81 0.69 0.61 0.59 0.60   (mean 0.84)
u std under QK-Norm             1.00 at every layer, times the learned gain²
```

So it is a **1.18× change in the mean evidence scale** — far smaller than feared — but it flattens a 2.3×
spread across depth that each layer's learned gain would then have to re-establish. τ_s = 2.37 reproduces
the trained gate scale exactly; the re-gate brackets it either side.

## What was decided, and what was built

Signed 2026-08-26 after a grill.

| decision | depth | state |
|---|---|---|
| ELR is the coordinate every norm-control gate is reported on | root | built: `mote/train/elr.py`, `w_norm`/`elr`/`rms_per_entry`/`rms_spread` in every log line, `lr_horizon --coord elr` |
| Muon vs Muon-SW re-run at matched ELR, **per matrix** | root | built: `--elr-trace-out` / `--elr-match`, `scripts/elr_optimizer_gate.sh`, `scripts/elr_report.py`. ~4 h GPU |
| QK-Norm into Relation, gated by a paired arm **plus** a τ_s/λ re-gate | root | built: `RelationCfg.qk_norm`, `--qk-norm`, `--tau-s`, `--lambda-init`, `scripts/qk_norm_gate.sh`. ~8 h GPU |
| Norm guard: trip when ‖W‖_F falls while lr is flat | root | built: `elr.NormGuard`, `--norm-guard` |
| A trip stops the run and **holds the queue** | root | built: `jobs.py` `held` state + `halted` flag, `mote train release` |
| Mid 2×2 → 2×3: measure the decay window, do not assume 0.2 | root | built: `--branch-decay-frac`. ~0.9 GPU-day, inside mid |
| Seed-noise calibration runs both metrics | — | both numbers above; the pre-launch arm still runs on fresh code |
| Thursday's queue drains untouched, then the new arms | — | nothing was front-queued |

Two implementation notes worth keeping:

* **Per-matrix rates without one group per matrix.** Muon's batched Newton-Schulz runs *before* its
  per-parameter apply loop, so a `param_lr` dict on the existing groups gives exact per-matrix learning
  rates at no throughput cost. One group per matrix would have turned ~5 kernel launches into 61.
* **`residual_proj` is zero-initialized.** `η_i = η^eff·‖W_i‖` is 0 for a zero matrix, which would freeze
  it for the whole run. It keeps the schedule's own lr until its first update gives it a norm.

Fixed in passing: `config.json` was written before every architecture flag was applied, so `--no-mbp`,
`--attention-main`, `--moe`, `--bf16-residual` and `--relation-window` never appeared in a run's readable
record. The checkpoint always carried the finished config, so resumes were right and only the file was
wrong.

## Still open

* Whether the collapse holds at Mote's architecture at all — that is what the matched-ELR pair answers,
  and it gates how far the ELR coordinate can be trusted.
* PRISM's 15/85 base-merge (2603.17074).
* 2607.12360's noise-regime diagnostic. Partly subsumed: logging ELR shows directly whether Muon's step
  anneals on its own, since under a flat lr the norm's growth decays ELR without the schedule doing
  anything.
