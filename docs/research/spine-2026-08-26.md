## 1. The headline mechanism is not the mechanism

mHC is *named* for constraining `H_res` to the Birkhoff polytope, and its own Tab. 1 attributes most
of HC's gain to `H_res`. Four groups have since measured otherwise, at scales closer to Mote's:

| source | finding |
|---|---|
| stream collapse 2606.03483 | freeze every `H_res` to identity except every 6th layer → performance **preserved, and better** on the medium model |
| Hyperloop 2604.21254 Tab. 6 | identity < **Sinkhorn** < data-dependent **diagonal**; a diagonal beats the doubly-stochastic matrix outright |
| sHC 2603.20896 §4.1 | "identity degeneration" — Birkhoff matrices concentrate around identity, abandoning the mixing |
| JPmHC 2602.18308 §7.1 | "**spectral stalling**": >75 % of the Jacobian's spectral mass sits at 0 under bistochastic skips, so ~75 % of directions get no gradient. Cayley beats implicit-Sinkhorn 1.13× exact-match, 1.27× eval loss at matched compute |

Three of the four are independent of each other. The Birkhoff machinery is **insurance against an
instability, not a source of gain** — and the entire KromHC / go-mHC / TBP-mHC / TEMPER / mHC-lite
parameterization literature is optimizing the cost of that insurance.

## 2. Constrain the property, not a proxy for it

sHC §5.1 splits the constraint into the two things it is doing:

> `B_n = A_n ∩ {H ≥ 0}` — an affine subspace (rows and columns sum to 1) intersected with non-negativity.

The **affine** part conserves the mean across streams; that is the identity-mapping property, and it
is what you want. **Non-negativity** is what bounds `‖H‖₂ ≤ 1` — sufficient, unnecessary, and the
exact part that forbids subtraction and drives the degeneration above.

Their Proposition 1: with `H_res = J + H_disp`, `J = (1/n)·11ᵀ`, `H_disp` in the zero-marginal
subspace, the two act on orthogonal subspaces (`J x⊥ = 0`, `H_disp x∥ = 0`), so

```
‖J + H_disp‖₂ = max(‖J‖₂, ‖H_disp‖₂) = max(1, ‖H_disp‖₂)
```

Bound `‖H_disp‖₂ ≤ 1` and you have mean conservation *and* the spectral bound, with negative entries
allowed. **`B_n ⊂ S_n`** — the Birkhoff polytope is strictly inside the spectral sphere, so choosing
the sphere cannot cost expressivity relative to mHC.

`mote/model/spine.py` implements `H_res = J + project(raw)` with `project` a flag, which makes every
variant in the literature a free control arm. Reproduced in our own code at n=4, 2048 random inputs
at 5σ (`tests/test_spine.py`):

| project | ‖H‖₂ max | rows→1 | cols→1 | negatives | cols/site |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spectral_sphere (sHC, **default**) | 1.0000 | ✓ | ✓ | **yes** | 9 |
| orthogonal (JPmHC) | 1.0000 | ✓ | ✓ | **yes** | 3 |
| sinkhorn (mHC) | **1.0090** | ✓ | **✗** | no | 16 |
| perm_convex (mHC-lite) | 1.0000 | ✓ | ✓ | no | 24 |
| diag (Hyperloop) | 1.0000 | ✗ | ✗ | no | 4 |
| none (HC) | **16.56** | ✗ | ✗ | yes | 16 |

Two rows are worth stating plainly. `sinkhorn` overshoots the bound it claims and its columns miss 1
— 20 Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations do not converge, exactly as mHC-lite §4.3 reported, now reproduced
here and pinned by a test so the control arm cannot quietly stop being a control. And `none` really
does explode, which is what makes it a control at all.

## 3. Why the spine is where it is

An H-Net has no long residual stream: the chunker resets it three times. HC's instability is a
deep-stack phenomenon (composite gain **3000** at 27B; mHC-lite showed the Sinkhorn drift is far
worse at 24 layers than 6), and Mote's stacks are 3, 18 and 3 layers with no arm having ever spiked.

So the spine runs at byte resolution, seven sites over one stream:

```
embed → ⟦enc L1⟧ → ⟦enc L2⟧ → ⟦enc L3⟧ → ⟦route → chunk → main(18L) → dechunk⟧ → ⟦dec L1..L3⟧ → head
```

with the main network keeping its own plain residual inside — different width (768 vs 512), different
resolution (~2968 chunks vs 16384 bytes). **No paper in this family crosses a resolution boundary**;
that part is ours and unvalidated.

Consequences worth naming:

* The spine is **7 sites deep regardless of the main network**, so taking Mote-96M → Mote-138M by
  depth (12 → 18 main layers) does not reopen the stability question the Birkhoff constraint exists
  for. The depth decision and the manifold decision are decoupled.
* `residual_proj` is **subsumed**: the chunk-stage site's `H_post` is the write path. That retires
  the model's only zero-norm parameter — the single case `ELRMatcher.apply` had to special-case.
* Seven sites is the extreme end of a granularity finding two papers reach independently: Hyperloop
  Tab. 5 has hyper-connections *after every loop* beating *after every layer* despite the latter
  using more compute and parameters, and the identity-freeze result above says the same.
* **HC's gains are a depth phenomenon and this spine is deliberately shallow.** It may measure as
  noise. That is an answer, not a failure.

## 4. Three things the implementation turned up

**(a) sHC's saturation constant does not transfer, because depth is spent differently.** sHC sets the
tanh bias to 4.0 so `Σ ≈ I` at init. But `sech²(4) = 0.0013` damps every gradient into the singular
values by 750×, so Σ sits pinned at 1 and `H_res = J + (I−J) = I` — reproducing by construction the
identity degeneration sHC exists to prevent.

| bias | tanh | per-site identity error | over 7 sites | gradient damping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 0.96403 | 3.60 % | 0.774 | 0.0707 |
| **3.0** | 0.99505 | 0.49 % | 0.966 | 0.0099 |
| 4.0 | 0.99933 | 0.07 % | 0.995 | 0.0013 |

`expand` uses 3.0; `frac` uses 4.0. Measured both ways: `frac`'s `b_res` gradients run 2.2e-2 against
`expand`'s 1e-3, so it has an order of magnitude of headroom to spend, and it needs the identity more
— its slices differ, so `(I−J)X ≠ 0` and the per-site attenuation actually compounds.

**(b) A mean read-out cannot see a mean-conserving mixer.** `dL/dH_res` comes out constant down the
destination axis, `H_res`'s columns sum to 1 by construction, and the two contract to **exactly**
zero. On a seven-site stack a plain `.mean(-2)` leaves the final site's `H_res` at a gradient of
2e-8 against 1e-3…6e-3 upstream — the last mixer is dead, because no non-uniform reader follows it.
`StreamExpand.collapse` is therefore a learned convex read, which also preserves scale, so HC's `√n`
init correction is not needed on the same line `_init_weights` already scales by `1/√n_res`.

**(c) `expand` is *exactly* the identity at init; `frac` is 0.4 % off.** With identical streams,
`(I−J)X = 0`, so whatever the singular values are doing cannot attenuate the residual — the affine
constraint earning its place. `frac`'s slices differ, so `tanh(4)⁷` shows up as a 0.4 % shrink.

Two collisions caught before they bit: `split_muon_params` routes on shape alone, so a 4×4
doubly-stochastic bias would have been **Newton-Schulz orthogonalised** (fixed with `_no_muon`); and
HC wants a `√n` init correction on exactly the modules `_init_weights` already scales by `1/√n_res`
(avoided by averaging rather than summing the streams).

## 5. Cost at Mote-138M (138,401,306 params)

Seven sites, D0=512, 16384 bytes, fp32 residual. Baseline spine residual: 224 MB.

| variant | extra params | total | residual | est. peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | — | 138.40M | 224 MB | ~5.04 GB |
| **Frac m=4** | 86,205 | 138.5M | **224 MB** | ~5.04 GB |
| **expand n=4, sHC** | 243,852 | 138.6M | 896 MB | ~5.70 GB |
| expand n=4, sinkhorn | 344,253 | 138.7M | 896 MB | ~5.70 GB |
| expand n=4, mHC-lite | 458,997 | 138.9M | 896 MB | ~5.70 GB |

Of 8.0 GB. Scaled from the measured 4.31 GB at Mote-96M — **not measured**, and the first thing to
do when the card frees is `mote.train.profile_step`.

The real risk is not memory: 7 of 42 sublayers leave the fused Triton `rms_norm_fn` path, and because
they sit at byte resolution they carry **42 % of residual elements** (58.7M vs 82.1M). `docs/shape.md`
records elementwise/dtype traffic at ~39 % of GPU time before that fusion landed. A fused spine
kernel is the answer (mHC's own TileLang kernels exist for exactly this, and cost them +6.7 %); it is
written against a measurement, not before one.

## 6. The arms

`scripts/spine_gate.sh`. ELR-matched on shared parameters via `--elr-match`, because the spine arm
and its control have different parameter sets and matched nominal lr is not matched ELR — the
confound that reopened the Muon vs Muon-SW freeze on 2026-08-26.

```
A0  control                       --elr-trace-out       Mote-138M, --spine off
A1  --spine frac   --spine-n 4    --elr-match A0        zero memory
A2  --spine expand --spine-n 4    --elr-match A0        +0.66 GB
A3  --spine expand --spine-project sinkhorn             only if A2 clears: sHC vs production mHC
```

**Gate:** endpoint `val_bpb` at matched seed. Seed noise 0.00025 bpb; the standing gates are
0.003/0.005. The literature's effect converts to roughly **−0.010 bpb** (−0.027 nats/token ÷ ~4
bytes/byte-token ÷ ln 2) — ~3× the gate and ~40× noise, so it is measurable if it is real.
Per-stream norm spread through `NormTracker` is a live diagnostic, not a gate: stream collapse is a
norm runaway, which is the coordinate the ELR work already instrumented.

## 7. What would make this wrong

* Seven sites may be too shallow for streams to differentiate. The one-site version is provably
  degenerate (the streams become two tensors); seven may not be enough more.
* sHC is one paper from March 2026 with zero production mileage. **DeepSeek-V4 (1.6T, with Muon) and
  Motif 3 (314B) both ship Sinkhorn mHC.** A3 exists to catch this.
* Crossing a resolution boundary is unprecedented in this family.
* Motif 3 anneals the `H_post` multiplier 2 → 1 against residual-stream outlier growth. `--spine-post-scale`
  exposes it; nothing yet schedules the anneal.

## 8. First harness numbers (CPU, Mote-13M, 1024 bytes)

Not the answer — the card is held by the E4 arm — but it validates the plumbing and flags one thing
the memory table hides:

| spine | params | B/s | vs off | flops/byte |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 10,870,110 | 1,601 | 1.000× | 16.6 M |
| frac n=4 | 10,843,134 | 1,101 | 0.688× | 18.2 M |
| expand n=4 | 10,897,606 | 1,403 | 0.876× | 20.4 M |

**`frac` is free in memory, not in compute.** Its read is a full m×m mix of slices where `expand`'s is
a 1×n reduction, so it adds FLOPs and an extra einsum while adding no bytes. On CPU at this size that
makes it the *slower* of the two; on the card, where `expand` pays for reading 4× the residual, the
ranking should invert. Which way it actually lands is one of the things the profile step in
`scripts/spine_gate.sh` exists to settle before 12 GPU-hours are spent.
